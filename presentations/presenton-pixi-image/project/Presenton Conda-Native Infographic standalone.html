01

The problem

Regulated estates cannot call home for a slide SaaS. Whether a disconnected on-prem stack already ships the equivalent is the load-bearing unknown. No story has entered implementation. Epic counts for this chain are not in this ledger.

Realization dates on this ledger: 2026-07-23 · 2026-08-02 · 2026-09-08.

02

The two-gate model

The PRD scores success on two gates that must both hold, because either one collapsing kills the product on its own axis — a JTBD shape written to catch a partial win early rather than declare victory on procurement alone.

Buyer-gate (binary, procurement-visible): every confirmed recipe upstream-merged on conda-forge, landed on the customer’s JFrog Artifactory mirror, assembled into an OCI image on the customer’s own registry, carrying a CycloneDX+SPDX SBOM, a cosign attestation, and a versioned /metrics schema with every build. None of this depends on a single end user ever opening the tool — it is a supply-chain and compliance gate a security team can clear on paper, independent of whether anyone likes the generated decks.

User-gate (behavioral, renewal-driving): one pilot customer clears a three-signatory acceptance checklist — CISO, platform-owner, and a named end-user lead, with a backup-signatory continuity clause so the checklist does not stall on one person changing roles mid-pilot — within a bounded go-live window, with a one-time extension available if the window is missed narrowly. The behavioral bar inside that checklist is “edit-not-rewrite” on most piloted decks, plus a Tier-1 per-refinement latency target. Missing either gate inside its window returns the whole program to Phase 0 scoping rather than limping forward on a missed target — the PRD treats a stalled pilot as a signal to re-scope, not a reason to extend indefinitely on hope.

04

Why full air-gap, not just on-prem

The PRD writes four hard, no-regression constraints into the architecture rather than leaving them as aspirations to be traded away under schedule pressure.

Zero LibreOffice in the runtime. Zero non-conda-forge packages in the runtime. Zero non-pixi build steps. Zero external CDN access at build or at runtime. Every channel/package resolution in the build pipeline routes through the *_BASE_URL env-var family; no recipe, build step, or CI job hardcodes a public URL, and the build CI runs inside the customer’s own perimeter against an internal JFrog Artifactory mirror — a dependency that is not allowlisted on that mirror fails the build outright rather than silently reaching past it. This is the same posture the PyForge factory already runs its own enterprise routing under (JFrog + internal-mirror auth chain, env-var driven, never committed config): the chain would inherit a pattern already proven at fleet scale, not invent a new one from a blank page.

Four framing decisions were locked early, before any build work, precisely so the air-gap boundary would not get renegotiated mid-project: the editable-PPTX fidelity bar is image-overlay plus extracted text shapes, matching upstream behavior rather than chasing native chart-object editability; the LLM provider strategy is locked to llmai’s existing env-var contract across all three tiers plus the dev-path bridge, with no source patch required; and the air-gap definition itself is locked to full air-gap — build CI inside the perimeter, every dependency allowlisted or the build fails, no partial or “mostly air-gapped” middle ground entertained anywhere in the design.

05

Non-goals, held on purpose

A Spec is as much what it refuses as what it commits to. Six items were named out of scope deliberately, not by oversight.

template-style-extractor is dropped entirely — upstream Presenton already ships LibreOffice-free template import with the identical legacy-format rejection this project had independently proposed building (see the discovery section in Act III). The end web UI of upstream Presenton stays out of scope; upstream owns the React/Next.js UI entirely, and this chain never touches it. A full JetBrains plugin is deferred past v1, which ships a docs-only one-pager instead. Upstreaming Chromium itself directly to conda-forge stays out of scope too — it is still stalled at a staged-recipes issue with no 2026 movement, and even the issue’s own opener doubts it is worth the effort; v1 vendors chrome-headless-shell via playwright-with-chromium instead, and direct upstreaming is Vision-tier at best. An SVG-to-DrawingML fidelity tier is net-new work with no existing library to build from — also Vision-tier. And knowledge-base integration beyond a prompt plus uploaded files is explicitly held against customer pressure as a v1 boundary: decks whose source material does not fit in a prompt and a handful of files stay out of scope until a later tier, no matter how reasonable a specific customer’s ask sounds in the moment.

06

Landing conditions

Four guardrails bound how the v1 scope is allowed to move, so scope creep has to clear a named check rather than happen by accretion.

Engineering complexity is honestly re-rated whenever v1 scope expands — already exercised once, rating recipes as medium-to-high effort, the clean-room work as high, and the overall platform/supply-chain surface as very-high, rather than leaving an optimistic estimate on the books after the scope grew. The CVE-response cycle is measured-and-conditional-on-JFrog-SLA, with a per-segment service-level target for upstream-patch, recipe-rebuild, and JFrog-allowlist-sync separately, not one assumed blended number. Brand-compliance enforcement thresholds are sized as a research spike, but the three-lane UX design itself — auto-fix, batched-review, ignored-with-strict-mode-opt-in — is a v1 commitment, not something deferred to a later release. And the boundary that “knowledge-base integration is Vision-tier, not v1” is held even against direct customer pressure, with v1 explicitly scoped to decks whose source material fits in a prompt plus uploaded files — a boundary the fixture-capture sequencing in the Test Strategy section is written to respect from day one, not retrofit later.

08

Exit 6: two coupled decisions

Exit 6 was added late, in the same revision pass that corrected Risk R3, because it bears directly on whether the product’s core differentiator still holds.

6(a), the Redmond contingency. Microsoft’s own disconnected stack — Azure Local disconnected operations, Microsoft 365 Local, and Foundry Local — went GA worldwide in February 2026, explicitly targeting government, healthcare, and finance sovereign/data-residency buyers. The PRD’s original risk framing had been keyed to Copilot reaching GCC/GCC-High/DoD/IL5 — that trigger already fired without collapsing the JTBD, because those tiers are Azure Government cloud, not air-gapped. The real trigger, the disconnected on-prem stack, was the load-bearing unknown, and it was chased down: Microsoft 365 Local’s own capability list names exactly three on-premises server products — Exchange Server, SharePoint Server, Skype for Business Server. The Office client apps, and with them any Copilot deck generation, are absent; Foundry Local is positioned explicitly as the AI layer, not the application layer. Risk R3 resolves to infrastructure-only, the third of the PRD’s three named outcomes — the mildest of the three, not the one that collapses the product.

The caveat, and why the exit stays open. This is documentary evidence. The story’s own acceptance criteria call for a Microsoft licensing/product conversation or hands-on access to a disconnected deployment — neither happened, and neither is something this repo can perform on its own. The exit is answered, not formally closed, and the other five exits are entirely untouched by this finding. The full account, the contradictory third-party source it corrects, and a per-exit state table live in the deferred-work ledger as DW-PRESENTON-1-6-1 and DW-PRESENTON-PHASE0-1 — the first tracked record this gate has ever had. The existing RSS/keyword-watch mechanism that should have caught the GA announcement is also flagged for a channel-coverage audit against Microsoft’s own product-doc channels, not just its roadmap/blog channels, since it appears to have missed the announcement entirely.

6(b), the memory-subsystem fork. mem0ai and fastembed-vectorstore are unconditional upstream Presenton dependencies, and neither has a conda-forge presence today. The confirmed recipe closure is five; whether it becomes seven depends entirely on this decision, which is still open. The architecture pre-wires both branches — a pixi feature flag and a Helm value, both defaulting off — so neither choice requires a later rework, but the branch itself has not been walked, and a clean no-op import path (dropping the feature without a Presenton-side source patch) has not been verified either way.

The two-plane paradigm

The architecture names exactly one swappable seam. Everything else is a fixed, air-gapped build plane.

The air-gapped build plane assembles fixed, pinned recipes into one pixi-locked environment; nothing in it varies per deployment. The LLM-provider plane is the one place configuration is allowed to differ: a Helm value picks a tier, and the app reads exactly two environment variables — CUSTOM_LLM_URL and CUSTOM_LLM_API_KEY, with optional OPENAI_BASE_URL/ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL passthrough — to reach it. No tier-specific code path is permitted to creep into the app on either side of that seam; the discipline is enforced by naming it a constraint in the architecture spine, not left as a convention teams might drift away from under deadline pressure.

Rendering and the clean-room export

Three of the five confirmed recipes replace closed-source pieces of upstream Presenton with clean-room, source-available components, discovered by reading upstream code directly rather than assuming its shape from an older draft.

The discovery pass found that LibreOffice, which an earlier draft placed on the template-import path, was gone from upstream entirely by the time of the 2026-07-25 re-verification — no ARG INSTALL_LIBREOFFICE, zero soffice references anywhere in the repo, confirmed by a direct code search rather than assumed. Template import now runs on pure standard library (zipfile+ElementTree) for DOCX/PPTX/XLSX, native ODF support, and pdfplumber (MIT) for PDF text — which is why template-style-extractor, an originally-planned sixth component, was dropped outright rather than built: upstream already ships the shape this project had independently proposed inventing, including the identical legacy-format rejection behavior. What remains closed-source is the export bundle (a roughly 6 MB minified Node script) and the PPTX assembler (a roughly 50 MB PyInstaller binary), both fetched as opaque release artifacts with no published source anywhere upstream makes available.

14

Upstream, verified directly

A dedicated technical research pass re-fetched upstream Presenton’s repository, Dockerfile, and dependency manifests directly rather than trusting the PRD’s original discovery pass at face value — and found upstream had moved.

Presenton itself is Apache-2.0, actively developed (pushed the same day this research ran), with thousands of stars and over a thousand forks — not an abandoned project this chain would be repackaging into a dead end. The monorepo splits a Next.js frontend, a FastAPI backend, and an Electron desktop build (out of scope for this OCP-server target) into separate directories. The runtime container itself confirmed several corrections to the original discovery: a pinned Debian-packaged Chromium build rather than a Puppeteer-managed download, Node.js 20 alongside the Python backend, and — again — zero LibreOffice anywhere in the build. The closed-source export bundle ships a new tagged release roughly every three days in the sampled window, repackaged from the old two-binary shape into one archive per platform, but its opacity is unchanged: still release-artifacts-only, still no published source, still the same clean-room rationale for presenton-export-node and pptx-assembler.

The most consequential correction: llmai itself. Presenton pins it to an exact version, not a floor, and that exact pin turned out to be several releases ahead of the version the PRD had originally cited — a three-month-old planning artifact drifting behind a dependency that ships new releases every few weeks. The env-var contract this whole chain depends on (CUSTOM_LLM_URL/CUSTOM_LLM_API_KEY) was independently re-confirmed by reading the actual config-loading code, not assumed from the PRD’s description of it.

22

Signed air-gapped image assembly

The image-assembly layer is a strict consumer, never a patcher.

It pulls the confirmed recipes as published, versioned conda artifacts by name and pin only — it never vendors or reaches into recipe internals, and recipe.yaml content itself stays out of this layer’s scope entirely, governed instead by conda-forge-expert (AD-19). The five, or seven, recipes assemble into a single pixi-locked environment, which pixitainer then consumes to build one reproducible OCI image, with zero external CDN access anywhere in the build and zero non-pixi build steps permitted to sneak in around it.

23

One provenance pass

Exactly one syft+cosign step runs per image build, never two disagreeing SBOMs for the same tag (AD-21).

The psycopg license flag (LGPL-3.0-only, a different obligation class than the Apache/MIT-dominated rest of the stack) is routed through this same provenance discipline for buyer legal review rather than assumed clean (Risk R7). It is the kind of compliance gate Warden already proves at fleet scale — 49 of 49 stories across 12 of 12 epics — so this chain would be consuming an already-proven pattern rather than inventing a new one from scratch.

24

The memory pre-wire, either way

The architecture does not wait on exit 6(b) to be buildable (AD-23).

A presenton-memory pixi feature and a values.memory.enabled Helm value are pre-wired end to end, both defaulting off. If exit 6(b) confirms a clean no-op import path with the feature dropped, the defaults simply stay as they are. If a Presenton-side source patch turns out to be required to add mem0ai and fastembed-vectorstore cleanly, that patch joins the same patch set as the export-pipeline recipes, and the decision becomes either “add both recipes” or a documented patch-maintenance acceptance — not a silent re-defer a second time, which is exactly the failure mode the pre-wiring exists to close off before it can happen.

The v1 platform additions

Five platform-level items ride alongside the core closure, each scoped explicitly rather than left implicit.

Brand-compliance enforcement is a three-lane UX framework: auto-fix at high confidence, batched review at mid-confidence, and ignore at low confidence with a strict-mode opt-in, thresholds still pending a Phase-0 spike. Agent orchestration reuses Presenton’s existing capability pinned to a specific release tag chosen at Phase 0, not a net-new build. The OCP deployment model

The Helm chart’s SecurityContext defaults to Restricted-SCC compatibility on every target cluster — no exceptions requested at install time (AD-24).

Restricted SCC is stricter than a documentation summary suggests once you read the implications through: the container tolerates an arbitrary, OpenShift-assigned UID at pod start rather than assuming root or a fixed UID, writing only to paths owned by a pre-allocated GID via the well-known chgrp -R 0 + chmod g=u convention. No privileged mode and no host-directory volume mounts are available to any design that assumed a privileged Chromium sidecar. A read-only root filesystem is not automatically implied by Restricted SCC itself — it is a common hardening layer many OCP clusters add on top — so the Helm chart is designed to support it as an opt-in, with writable scratch space via emptyDir or a PVC, rather than assuming either posture by default.

Steward — 231 of 263 stories, 56 of 62 epics on its own live work — is the station named to operate this once an image exists to deploy. It has not started, because there is nothing yet to operate.

The four fixture sets

The Test Strategy names four fixture sets and one documented architectural seam: exactly one of the four is captured online, everything else runs fully air-gapped.

SetPathWho, and how
1 — authortests/fixtures/upstream-baseline/the package-author, online, at exit 3; byte/structural equivalence for non-image output, SSIM at or above 0.99 for image equivalence — explicitly a tolerance, never byte-equivalence for images
2 — maintainertests/drift/the recipe-maintainer, weekly online cron; a structured drift report separates breaking drift (the clean-room reimpl will diverge) from benign drift (a cosmetic upstream change, still semantically correct)
3 — day-2tests/operational/the operator, shipped inside the OCI image itself and run in the air-gapped target environment; smoke deck, credential rotation, mark-broken-pinned-deps
4 — day-0tests/install/the operator, before first deploy; registry-reachable, secrets-present, manifest-validates, JFrog auth working, GGUF present if Tier 2 or 3

The breaking-versus-benign classifier itself is specified down to the routing logic: four synthetic-release fixtures (a benign patch, a breaking dependency bump, a breaking API change, a no-op re-release) prove the classifier sorts correctly; a deterministic, CI-gated allowlist absorbs known-benign patterns without ever firing an issue; anything ambiguous — fixture-only changes or borderline CSS/markup edits — routes instead to a human-review queue with a forty-eight-hour SLA, and the harness itself fires an escalation if that queue’s depth exceeds five items or its oldest item ages past seventy-two hours. A drift-harness step failure is designed to exit clean from the cron job with a warning annotation rather than ever breaking anyone’s build.

Upstream drift defense

Several clean-room artifacts are that many divergence vectors once upstream Presenton ships its next major version — the plan treats that as a certainty to defend against, not a risk to hope around (Risk R2).

The same fixture-capture script exit 3 runs once by hand becomes a reusable, weekly, online CI workflow that re-captures upstream behavior and diffs it against the committed baseline, auto-filing an issue on breaking drift. That online workflow never shares a runner or a network path with the air-gapped build workflow (AD-22) — enforced at the CI-runner and network-policy level, not left to convention, so a drift-detection job cannot become the accidental hole in the air-gap it exists to protect. The same path-watch also triggers a fixture-capture refresh directly whenever upstream’s export directory changes, rather than waiting for the weekly cadence to notice on its own.
