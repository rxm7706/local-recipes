ACT I
The problem and the model
friction to insight
01

The problem

Enterprises want open-source culture inside the firewall without a second toolchain. The stranded gists (Constitution, pixi root, toolchain spec) never landed as a running platform. Atlas hosts the estate narrative; Unity is not a capability of the cf_atlas pipeline.

Realization dates on this ledger: 2026-07-23.

Every team building Python data products inside a large enterprise independently re-solves the same six problems: native and Python dependency resolution together, offline reproducibility, supply-chain compliance, OpenShift deployment, cross-platform testing, and letting another team contribute without breaking anything. The organization pays for the difference forever — in onboarding time, audit effort, duplicated internal libraries, and the quiet conclusion that cross-team sharing is not worth the trouble.

Three substantial artifacts already existed but were never landed in a repository, and had measurably drifted from what they claimed: a 37 KB spec-kit Constitution, a 1,726-line working pixi root, and a toolchain spec pairing a Pixi orchestrator root with a PDM/pip-tools PEP 751 lock format. This chain’s job was to absorb what research confirmed, correct what it falsified, and supply what was missing — principally the human contribution model and the provenance chain.

The mandate carries a live clock, not just a technical wish list: the EU Cyber Resilience Act entered into force 10 December 2024, and its vulnerability-reporting obligations begin 11 September 2026. “Know what you ship, continuously” stops being aspirational good practice and becomes a dated legal duty on that date — Act V returns to exactly this clock once the capability map below is on the table.

A second clock runs alongside it, quieter but just as real: the three intake artifacts have already measurably drifted since they were authored between January and May 2026. Pixi itself has moved fourteen minor versions past the working root’s hard exact pin, a pin that blocks installation outright rather than merely warning about it. The work either lands soon, on artifacts this fresh, or the whole intake set needs re-verification from scratch before anyone can trust it again.

02

Who this is for

Eight Jobs To Be Done drove every capability in Act II — each one is a thing a real person in the enterprise is trying to accomplish, not a feature request.

  • Ship a data product without becoming a toolchain administrator first.
  • Install a colleague’s internal library and have it work on the first attempt.
  • Reproduce an environment offline, on a different OS, months later, byte-for-byte.
  • Contribute a fix to shared code owned by another team, and have it land in days.
  • Answer “what is in our estate, and is any of it being exploited right now?” continuously, with evidence, without a person walking N repositories.
  • Set a standard once and have it hold, without policing it by hand.
  • Deploy behind a firewall as the ordinary path, not as a project.
  • Onboard a new engineer without transferring tribal knowledge.

Four audience boundaries are just as deliberate, inferred from the Dream and the intake artifacts rather than confirmed with a stakeholder, and named as an assumption rather than asserted as fact:

Not a v1 userWhy
Teams outside Python/dataPython-first by mandate; a front-end-only or JVM team is not a v1 audience — Node tooling exists only in service of Python-backed applications.
Organizations wanting hosted SaaSUnity is a platform an enterprise runs, not a service it subscribes to.
Teams needing a service catalog/portalThat is Backstage’s job; Unity emits catalog-consumable facts, an adopter integrates rather than migrates.
Single-team projectsUnity’s cost is justified by cross-team sharing — one team with one service should not adopt it.

04

The Inner-Source model

Open-source culture and practices, run inside the enterprise instead of across the public internet. Chosen standards once, a shared toolchain, faster delivery, and consistency by construction rather than by a wiki page nobody re-reads.

Where pyforge-genesis installs the operating model — the crew, the stations, the planning chain this poster itself is built through — Unity Data Stack is the platform an enterprise would run on it. Genesis bootstraps a new Unity instance; this Spec does not assume Genesis exists yet, so bootstrapping a fresh Unity is explicitly out of scope for v1, carried in the Spec’s own Non-goals as a v2 dependency — Act VI’s closing sections return to the full Non-goals list.

The Dream names four Kinships this chain binds to, none of them yet built: pyforge-genesis (the installer that would bootstrap Unity instances), pyforge-warden (the Security/Compliance agents the intake toolchain spec already sketched, and the real reason CAP-6 integrates rather than reimplements), enterprise-airgap (the deployment posture CAP-8 answers), and packaging-factory (the conda-native package supply this very repository already runs, tracking 7864 recipes).

The intake toolchain spec already sketched an agent role matrix — Architect, Developer, DevOps, Security, Compliance — that prefigures this same crew before the crew existed. That is one of the findings this Spec confirms rather than falsifies: the shape was right even though the mechanism underneath one of its roles, described in Act V, was not.

05

What already existed

Nothing here started from a blank page — and nothing here was accepted unread either. Three gists, stranded since early 2026, were rediscovered in a routine gist audit on 2026-07-23 and became the raw material this Spec confirms, corrects, or supplies against.

The Constitution supplied the requirement spine — fourteen Articles this Spec traces every functional requirement back to. The working root supplied a genuinely complete pixi workspace shape at scale, roughly two hundred tasks covering the full local lifecycle — proof the pattern could be built, not just described, and simultaneously proof of its own risk: a surface that large is unlearnable without a small, stable, publicly-named subset (FR-60, Act IV). The toolchain spec supplied the compliance agent-role thinking this Spec absorbs into CAP-6, and one load-bearing claim — a specific pdm export flag — that turned out not to exist. Act V returns to that finding in full once the capability map is on the table.

A cameo exists too: a “Unity Knowledge Stack” infographic drafted in the sentinel Design session, dated 18 April 2026 — evidence the idea had already been sketched visually once before this chain existed, not a design asset this poster reuses.

07

The workspace substrate (CAP-1)

A platform engineer declares, in one Workspace root, the platform matrix, the package channels, the minimum system requirements, and the set of Packages. Adding a Package requires editing exactly one place (FR-1); no dependency version string is duplicated anywhere else (FR-4).

FR-2 pins the toolchain version as a floor with a tested ceiling, never an exact equality — a direct correction of the intake root’s hard requires-pixi exact-equality pin, already fourteen minor versions behind current at authoring time, which blocks every current install outright. FR-3 requires Environments declared minimal — production, dr, oss — to actually contain nothing but what they declare, with installed size measured against a documented ceiling; the intake root’s fat base dependency block, inherited by every Environment including the ones declared minimal, is exactly the defect this closes.

FR-9 is the one requirement that changes the intake design rather than merely correcting it: the twelve Stages become a first-class concept distinct from Environments, so the number of distinct solved Environments is bounded by genuine dependency variation, not by how many Stage names exist. The intake root declared roughly twenty Environments in which five were byte-identical and three more were separately identical — twelve Stages collapsing to roughly four real dependency sets, eight redundant solves paid for on every resolve. FR-7 keeps one deliberately non-deployable Environment anyway — a cross-stack compatibility check that composes the full mandated library set purely to catch conflicts before they reach a Domain — and FR-8 requires every excluded Package to record why: airflow-server (a SQLAlchemy <2.0 conflict with the orchestrator) and sharepoint-mcp-server (a pyjwt conflict) both carry forward as documented exclusions rather than silent gaps.

08

The one authoritative lock (CAP-2)

AD-2 makes exactly one Workspace Lock authoritative: pixi.lock resolves conda and PyPI packages together in one solve, with hashes, committed to version control (FR-10). The PEP 751 pylock.toml export and the air-gapped Offline Bundle (FR-13) are both generated from it and drift-checked against one pinned commit SHA per release — never hand-edited, never a second resolution input.

FR-11 pins this down precisely: multi-platform coverage must be proven by materialization, never assumed from a format’s own claim about itself — the exact discipline that would have caught the toolchain spec’s falsified pdm export flag before it ever reached a build script. FR-12 keeps the Exported Lock honest the same way: generated from, and drift-checked against, one pinned Workspace Lock commit SHA, failing the gate on any mismatch — and it flags, explicitly as an assumption, that the Workspace Lock is authoritative with the export derived (pixi-primary); the reverse direction and a split-by-tier variant remain live alternatives. The Spec notes this also resolves the PRD’s own open question OQ-1, its single most consequential decision, through AD-2.

FR-14 keeps mirror routing to environment variables only, with no edit to any committed manifest — the intake root’s CONDA_CHANNEL_ALIAS / PIP_INDEX_URL / UV_INDEX_URL / GHE_HOST design, kept intact. FR-15 and FR-16 are corrections, not confirmations: the intake toolchain spec declares its own Token Isolation Rule and then violates its spirit with a credential interpolated directly into an extra-index-urls URL and an --index-url interpolation in CI; FR-16’s per-host credential attachment closes the known JFROG_API_KEY unconditional-injection defect already recorded in the enterprise-airgap Dream, this time at the platform level rather than left to each Package. FR-17 closes a smaller but real gap: the intake root holds ten packages back from automatic updating in an inline command comment with no recorded rationale at all.

09

Local equals CI (CAP-3)

AD-9 turns every gate check into a named task with a globally unique name: CI invokes task names only, never an inline tool call, never inline installation, never an ad-hoc environment mutation. FR-18 requires a parity test that asserts the local and CI check-sets are identical and fails on divergence — and the Spec measures its own promise directly: the green-local/red-CI rate is itself a tracked success signal, SM-3.

FR-19 through FR-22 name what “everything CI runs” actually covers: linting, formatting and static type checking across Python and configuration formats; coverage thresholds with real numbers behind them — 100% for asset-producing code, an 80% minimum everywhere else — and a decrease relative to the base branch fails the same gate; and security scanning implemented through the same Compliance Report CAP-6 produces, not a second, competing mechanism. FR-23 requires pre-commit hooks to mirror a genuine subset of the full gate, never a cheaper, divergent one.

FR-21’s test-first discipline — new capability lands with its tests, a bug fix lands with a test that fails without the fix — is enforced by review rather than by automation; automated test-before-implementation detection is flagged as an assumption, not a solved problem (OQ-13). FR-25 adds a behavioural test tier selectable by tag, already present in the intake root’s behave suite with @smoke/@integration tags — but conspicuously absent from the Constitution’s own Article III, which the Spec flags as needing its own amendment to catch up with what the working root already proved out.

10

The Constitution as code (CAP-4)

AD-8 is the machine-classification mechanism the Constitution needs to stop being a document nobody re-reads: every Mandate carries a stable identifier and a classification of exactly platform-invariant or domain-default. An unclassified Mandate, or a check with no declared Mandate behind it, fails the Quality Gate outright — there is deliberately no third, ungoverned category (FR-26).

This is the central new work of the whole capability, and it is a correction, not a ratification: the Constitution as inherited declares itself uniformly “immutable” and “non-negotiable”, which directly conflicts with Data Mesh principle 4 — federated computational governance assumes some Mandates are locally overridable by design, not zero. Without the invariant/default split, Unity would be centrally imposed rather than genuinely innersource, no matter how well everything else here works.

FR-27 requires every Domain Default override to carry a recorded decision — rationale, alternatives, consequences — enumerable so a reader can list every active override and why it exists. FR-28 requires every failing check to name the exact Constitution clause it enforces. FR-29 makes the enforcement gap itself visible: a coverage report distinguishes Mandates that are automatically enforced from those that rely on human review, and a Mandate claimed as enforced with no corresponding check fails that same report. FR-30 requires the Constitution to carry its own semver, ratified date, amended date and next-review date — the inherited Constitution carries its own version stamp, with a next-review date already five months overdue at the time this Spec was written, which is precisely the kind of drift this requirement exists to surface automatically rather than by accident.

ACT III
Contribution and compliance
the human layer, then the machine layer
11

Trusted Committers and the scaffold (CAP-5)

CAP-5 is the Spec’s human-contribution model, and it was found missing independently from two separate research angles: the Constitution requires “at least one human approval” and never says whose, and the toolchain spec’s own agent role matrix omits every feedback-loop role. For a platform whose entire premise is cross-team co-contribution, the social layer was essentially unspecified.

FR-33 names a Trusted Committer for every Package, auto-requested as reviewer on any PR touching it, with documented responsibilities and an expected response window — supplying what the Constitution’s own Article VIII leaves undefined. FR-34 gives an outside contributor one documented path in: finding the owner, the branch convention, required tests, the review expectation, and what happens when the Trusted Committer does not respond — borrowed directly from InnerSource Commons practice, which the intake set never referenced at all.

FR-35’s branch and commit enforcement surfaces its own small, honest tension: the Constitution mandates Gitflow with develop as the default branch, which conflicts with this very host repository’s trunk-based main convention. Unity is a separate repository, so there is no direct collision — but the choice is flagged to be re-confirmed rather than silently inherited. FR-37 sets a hard bar for the scaffolding templates this same Spec commits to: a freshly generated Package or Data Product must pass the Quality Gate immediately, with no manual fixes, covering at minimum a shared library, a service, and a Data Product. FR-38 pairs the innersource proof with its own counter-signal — cross-team contribution and internal-fork/duplicate rates are both measured, feeding directly into Success signals SM-2 and SM-C1: reuse trending up only counts as proof while the fork counter-metric does not rise in step.

13

Data Products: Raw → Curated → Consumption (CAP-7)

CAP-7 gives each Domain ownership of its own Data Products, layered through three stages with an enforced naming convention and a structured metadata contract at each layer. This is, honestly, the part of the intake Constitution that was already most complete and most faithful to its own source architecture — the requirements here mostly ratify it rather than correct it.

FR-48 requires each Data Product to belong to exactly one Domain, with cross-Domain consumption only through published interfaces, never direct datastore access. FR-49’s three-Layer model (Raw / Curated / Consumption) is recorded as a deliberate Unity convention — the Data Mesh source material itself is silent on internal layer naming, so this is Unity choosing a convention rather than inheriting one. FR-50 enforces an <domain>_<layer>_<entity>_<verb> Asset naming pattern; FR-51 requires structured owner/domain/layer/frequency metadata queryable across the whole Workspace, so “what does this Domain publish?” is a generated answer, not a maintained document.

One reference Domain — customer — is implemented end to end as the pattern every other Domain follows (FR-54), exercising all three layers, publishing a contract, and passing every gate. It is exactly what the FR-37 scaffolding templates generate for a new Domain, so adopting the pattern and reading the reference implementation are the same exercise. The remaining ten Domains this platform would eventually need are, deliberately, flagged as an assumption rather than committed v1 scope — one worked example plus a proven, generated scaffold changes the build effort by roughly an order of magnitude versus building all eleven up front (OQ-2, revisited in Act V).

ACT IV
Deployment posture
air-gap first, not air-gap eventually
14

Air-gap parity and secrets (CAP-8)

CAP-8’s bar is high on purpose: every capability available with public network access must also be available in Air-Gap Mode, or declare in writing why not. Offline is treated as the design default, not a mode bolted on afterward — SM-6 targets 100% parity across the enumerated capability list, with any exception explicit, never silently degraded.

A Stage’s Data Classification bounds which datastores and which network posture it may be configured against — a Stage classified below Restricted simply cannot be configured against a datastore holding restricted data (FR-58), and Stages carrying restricted data carry access logging by requirement, not by convention. Deployment itself is declarative and reconciled to runtime state, promoted between Stages under that Stage’s own policy (FR-56); a Stage with a manual-approval policy cannot auto-promote. Secrets are never committed and are validated present at service startup rather than discovered missing at runtime; FR-57 fails an automated check the moment a secret-shaped string lands in the repository, and a service missing a required secret fails fast, naming it, rather than failing obscurely at first use.

FR-58 also names the largest unbacked assertion the whole intake set carried: the Constitution asserts PII masking, retention, right-to-deletion and audit logging — with no mechanism specified anywhere for any of them. This Spec does not silently drop that ambition; it scopes it. v1 enforces Data Classification at the configuration boundary only — which datastore, which network — rather than performing content-level inspection. Content-level PII detection and masking is named explicitly as v2 candidate scope (OQ-8), not quietly abandoned.

15

One local stack, one task API (CAP-9)

CAP-9 closes the loop AD-9 opens: a developer starts, stops and inspects the full local service stack — aggregate and per-service — with single commands, over a small, stable public task API.

FR-59 requires status to report actual service health, not merely that a process exists; a health check that only confirms a PID is running is treated as no check at all, and the aggregate start brings services up in dependency order. FR-60 makes the task surface itself a contract: removing or renaming a public task is a breaking change requiring a recorded decision, exactly the discipline AD-9 already applies to every gate task in Act II. This directly answers the working root’s own proven risk: it already names four tasks — start, stop, status, verify — as its “Agent & Developer Public API” out of roughly two hundred total. That was an excellent instinct in the intake root; this Spec makes it a requirement instead of leaving it as a convention someone could quietly widen.

Taken together, CAP-1 through CAP-9 describe a platform where the same small vocabulary of named tasks — not ad-hoc scripts, not tribal knowledge — is how a Workspace is declared, how it is locked, how it is checked, how it is governed, how it is contributed to, how it is proven compliant, how its Data Products are built, how it is deployed, and how it is run locally.

What the research falsified

The intake toolchain spec’s flagship lock command does not exist. The exact line was pdm export --format pylock --override-platform=linux --override-platform=macos --override-platform=windows — verified 25 July 2026, directly against the tool, not assumed from the gist’s own claim.

pdm export has no --override-platform flag at all. Platform targeting on PDM actually lives on pdm lock --platform, and the export format token is pylock.toml, not what the gist wrote. A working alternative does exist — uv export --format pylock.toml — but the intake spec’s exact mechanism, as written, is unimplementable. Compounding this, PEP 751 itself does not guarantee multi-platform lockfiles at all: it uses environment markers, not a cross-compilation guarantee, so the “Cryptographic Predictability” outcome the intake spec promised had no verified mechanism behind it whatsoever.

This single falsified claim is the empirical grounding for AD-2 and AD-3 together (Act II) — the Spec replaces an unverified format guarantee with gate-verified materialization (FR-11), precisely because trusting the format was already shown to fail once. It is also the reason the Spec’s own Assumptions still flag the Pixi-primary lock architecture as needing explicit human ratification rather than treating architecture’s resolution as automatically final: a Spec that had simply inherited the toolchain spec’s claims wholesale would have carried this same defect forward into a build.

The clock that is real

Not every deadline in a Spec is aspirational. The EU Cyber Resilience Act’s vulnerability-reporting obligations begin 11 September 2026, with the main body of obligations following on 11 December 2027.

“Know what you ship, continuously” stops being good practice and becomes a dated legal duty on that first date. SM-5 is written against exactly this calendar: compliance latency — the time from a vulnerability’s publication to a determination of estate impact — is measured in minutes, ahead of the reporting-obligation deadline, not caught up to it after the fact.

This is why CAP-6’s delivery mechanism matters as much as its FR count: integrating an already-shipping compliance gate (Warden, Act III) rather than building one from scratch is the difference between a plan that could plausibly meet this clock and one that could not, given how little runway is left between when this Spec was written and when the obligation itself begins.

Constraints and guardrails

Four guardrails sit underneath every capability in Act II, stated once here rather than repeated inside each one.

  • Safety. Automated remediation never merges without human review (FR-47). Automated enforcement fails closed — an unevaluable gate is a failing gate, never a passing one.
  • Privacy. Restricted data never leaves a Stage classified for it (FR-58). Secrets never enter version control, lockfiles, logs, or command lines (FR-15, FR-57).
  • Cost. Environment count is bounded by genuine dependency variation, not by Stage naming (FR-9) — every distinct Environment is a solve, an install and a cache entry, paid on every machine and every CI run. Minimal Environments must be genuinely minimal (FR-3).
  • Dependency policy. All package installation goes through the workspace manager; direct installer invocation is prohibited. Exceptions require a recorded decision (FR-27).
