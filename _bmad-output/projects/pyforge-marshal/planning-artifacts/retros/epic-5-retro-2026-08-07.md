# Epic 5 Retrospective: Fleet visibility

**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Epic:** 5 — Fleet visibility (6/6 stories done: 5.1–5.6)
**Facilitator:** Claude (direct synthesis, not full party-mode simulation — condensed per operator preference, same format as Epic 2/3/4)

## Summary

"What is running?" is now one command instead of five: `marshal status` gives one row per loop home, derived entirely from journals/run state, never a hand-maintained file (5.1); drills into one run's own story sequence, gate verdicts, and open intents (5.2); surfaces escalations first with the reason and artifact a decision needs (5.3); reconciles the tracked sprint ledger against git's own durable-merge evidence and names disagreement in either direction, tagged by confidence (5.4); folds in unpushed-work evidence from the repo's own existing detector so a home is never reported clean while carrying real local-only work (5.5); and routes the repo's detector registry through the same front door, with the first real (if deliberately scoped-down) step toward resolving shared context once per dispatch instead of per command (5.6). All 6 stories merged to `main` (PRs #283–#288); all 6 specs are durably promoted to tracked `planning-artifacts/specs/` — 5 via a real `marshal deploy promote` run (zero findings, clean verdict), one (5.1) by hand after `promote`'s own detection missed it — see "What was rough" below, a live and immediately-relevant confirmation of Story 5.4's own central finding.

## What went well

- **Story 5.4's own review became a live, load-bearing correctness check on the story's OWN design, not just its implementation.** The implementing engineer's live smoke test against this repo's real ledger surfaced 5 suspicious discrepancies; rather than accepting the implementation as shipped, the Blind Hunter review was explicitly tasked with independently investigating whether the AC's own symmetric "both directions reported as a discrepancy" framing was actually sound — and confirmed a genuine design flaw: this repo's own dominant squash-merge landing convention is invisible to git-subject archaeology, so one whole discrepancy direction is fundamentally unreliable. The fix (tagging every discrepancy with a `confirmed`/`unconfirmed` confidence level, not silencing either direction) is the SAME lesson Story 5.4 itself exists to teach, applied recursively to its own output. This is the clearest evidence yet that "review the diff" and "review the spec's own claims against reality" are both necessary, and adversarial review should target both.
- **The two-reviewer convergence pattern kept finding real bugs, though at a lower clean-convergence rate than Epic 4 (2 of 6 stories vs. 6 of 10) — worth naming honestly rather than inflating.** 5.3 and 5.5 had both reviewers land on the identical top finding independently (a field-gating bug in each case — escalation fields leaking onto non-escalated states in 5.3, unpushed-work evidence being suppressed on degraded rows in 5.5). The other four stories (5.1, 5.2, 5.4, 5.6) had reviewers find DIFFERENT real, valid top findings rather than converging — still valuable (nothing shipped un-reviewed), but a genuinely different signal than "the same bug was so obvious two independent reviewers couldn't miss it." Both patterns are useful; they answer different questions.
- **Reuse discipline held on genuinely deep primitives.** 5.4 reused `bmad_loop.sprintstatus.load` (the SAME parser the harness itself already trusts for the identical YAML shape) rather than a second hand-rolled reader; 5.5 reused the EXISTING `scripts/unpushed_work_check.py` detector rather than re-deriving its own branch-vs-remote diff; 5.6 routed to `scripts/detectors.py` rather than reimplementing detector discovery. Every story in this epic that touched an existing detector or parser extended it, never duplicated it.

## What was rough

- **A real, live promotion gap was discovered during closeout itself: Story 5.1's own PR (#283) was merged via `--squash` (my one deviation from the `--merge` convention this session otherwise used consistently for every other PR), and `marshal deploy promote`'s own `merged_story_keys` detection — confirmed by Story 5.4's own review to have exactly this blind spot — silently never recognized 5.1 as durably merged.** `marshal deploy promote` cleanly promoted 5.2–5.6 in one pass but left 5.1 out of BOTH `promoted` and `already_promoted`; recovered by hand, then independently confirmed via `marshal status --reconcile-ledger` correctly reporting it as `unconfirmed` rather than a false alarm or a silent gap — the tool this epic just shipped catching a real, self-inflicted process mistake on its very first live use after merging.
- **Story 5.6 required genuine, careful scoping-down under time pressure.** Its AC literally asks for shared context "resolved once and threaded to" four command verbs, including three large, already-hardened commands from prior epics. Retrofitting all three internals in one story would have been the highest-risk change of the whole epic; the AC's own "Q-15/Q-16 stay open" qualifiers gave real textual permission to narrow scope to "wire the plumbing, prove it end-to-end for the one new verb" — a defensible call, but one that required real judgment rather than a mechanical reading of the AC.
- **This story's own dispatch-mechanism change (5.6) was the highest-blast-radius diff of the epic** — `cli/main.py`'s shared dispatch is on the path of every `marshal` subcommand, not just the new one. The review caught a real security-adjacent gap here (an unvalidated project slug reading a policy file with none of the traversal/`PermissionError` hardening already established elsewhere for the identical lookup) — a good outcome, but a reminder that touching shared infrastructure deserves proportionally more scrutiny than touching one command's own internals, which every story in this epic that touched `cli/main.py`/`core/status.py` (5.1 first established both, then every subsequent story extended them) should keep in mind.

## Action items

1. Keep running Blind Hunter + Edge Case Hunter in parallel on every story — even at a lower clean-convergence rate this epic, every single story had at least one real, patch-worthy finding; the pattern has zero false-negative epics so far (12 of 16 stories across Epic 4+5 needed a real fix).
2. When a review is prompted with a specific, already-observed anomaly (as 5.4's was, given the engineer's own live smoke-test result) — DIRECT the reviewer to investigate it explicitly rather than leaving it to be rediscovered incidentally. This produced the epic's single most valuable finding.
3. Land future PRs with `--merge`, never `--squash`, without exception — this epic's own tooling (5.4) now makes a squash-merge slip immediately visible (`unconfirmed`, never silently missing) rather than a silent gap, but avoiding the slip in the first place is still cheaper than detecting and recovering from it.
4. Story 5.6's own deliberate scope narrowing (context plumbing wired, three existing commands' internals NOT retrofitted) is a real, tracked follow-up, not a closed question — a future epic/story should decide whether `factory spin`/`status`/`land` are worth migrating onto `MarshalContext` for real, per the epic's own still-open Q-15/Q-16.
