## What went well

- **The contract froze at 1.1 and held.** The `ComplianceReport` schema, verdict lattice, and exit-code contract survived the whole epic; even 1.9's resolved-scan-set schema addition was backward-compatible. This is the foundation every later epic depended on.
- **The fail-closed teeth were in from the start** — the socket-deny harness (1.2) and the no-scan guard (1.7) are what make Warden never false-green, and they were proven on the tool itself.
- **Predictable velocity by story shape:** 1.9 (manifest discovery / IO-matrix, ~137 min) ran heavy; the identity / renderer / guard stories were light (1.8 ~35, 1.6 ~49, 1.7 ~67).

## What was harder than it should have been

- **1.7 escalation:** an uncommitted `pixi.toml`/`pixi.lock` in the target tree blocked the auto-merge; resolved by committing separately then cherry-picking the story. A pre-launch clean-tree guard would have prevented it.
- **1.9's spec was lost** to the Tier-3 gitignored paper-trail gap (the worktree auto-cleaned after merge, taking the spec with it) — the first clear instance of a recurring problem.

## Lessons

1. The bmad-loop **dev** phase was reliable across all 9 stories; the friction was at **merge/cleanup**, not in the code.
2. The gitignored `implementation-artifacts/` paper trail is fragile — specs vanish on worktree teardown.
