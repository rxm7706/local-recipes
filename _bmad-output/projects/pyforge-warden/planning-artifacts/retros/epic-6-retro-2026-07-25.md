## What went well

- **The frozen-schema scope-check held on every gate.** 6.1 froze `report-schema.json` / `models.py` / `verdict.py`; every subsequent producer (6.2–6.9) was scope-checked to confirm a *zero* diff against that trio, and all came back clean. Cheap guard, real assurance that no axis widened the contract.
- **Adversarial review caught genuine defects** — e.g. 6.3's alias-collision silent-misroute; and across the axes, the reviewers repeatedly found real bugs before merge.
- **Model tiering worked** — sonnet default, opus for the two hard stories (6.5, 6.9); no quality regression traced to tier.
- **The suppression engine composes** — 6.8's baseline/grandfathering is a second producer over Epic 3's *same* waiver engine, and 6.9's actuation payload flows only into a frozen pass-through slot (the "independent sibling" discipline from Epic 4). The architecture held under extension.

## What was harder than it should have been

- **bmad-loop idle-strand was the dominant friction.** A mid-response API "Connection closed" parks the session until the ~4M-token cap fires, then defers. It hit **6.8 (dev-1)** and **6.9 (BOTH dev attempts → deferred)**.
- **6.9 required manual recovery.** The stalled session had *committed* its work (`f8e1648d2c`) before dropping; the story was deferred but the commit survived on the story branch. Recovered by hand: frozen-trio scope-check + full suite + the adversarial review the stall skipped, then a labeled merge.
- **6.2 landed at the 3-review-cycle cap** ("did not converge", 20+ fixes applied) — a genuine follow-up review of this compliance-critical `license.py` remains advisable.

## Lessons

1. **Idle-strand is the #1 process gap** — fix belongs in bmad-loop (idle timeout / keepalive / shorter cap), not per-run babysitting. The external strand-watchdog was an effective stopgap only.
2. **A stalled-but-committed story is deterministically recoverable** — the dev commit survives on `bmad-loop/<run>/<story>`; verify + review + `git merge --no-ff` with the exact merge subject the dashboard keys on.
3. **Verify a hypothesized bug against the validation layer before patching** — on 6.9 I proposed a guard for an `IndexError` that turned out unreachable (`Finding.__post_init__` validates the id family); I reverted the dead defensive code and merged the original. Adversarial *verification* is as load-bearing as hunting.
