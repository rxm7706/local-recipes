Traps met cutting Platform CI's service wait (2026-11-27, PR #2429; steward Story 117.31): (1) A job's step list hides where "Initialize containers" goes. Read the job's own log (gh api repos/<repo>/actions/jobs/<id>/logs) for the docker pull, create and start lines and the "Waiting for all services to be ready" group. Platform CI's 21 to 27 s split into about 7 s of pulls, 5 s of creates and 13.4 s of waiting. (2) The wait was Docker's health interval, not the service: probed every 10 s, PostgreSQL's first probe came 10 s after start, and the runner, backing off 2, 3 and 7 s between its own checks, saw it healthy 13 s after start. --health-start-period 30s --health-start-interval 1s (Docker 25 and later) probes every second while a service starts, and failures in that window do not count against its retries. Lowering --health-interval alone would let a slow initdb spend the retries and mark the service unhealthy. (3) A service option's effect can be checked without pulling its image: run any local image with a health command that turns true 3 s after start. It read healthy after 10.5 s at the old options and 3.3 s at the new. Pulling postgres or redis is a download, which needs the operator's yes. (4) A workflow-only change is proven on the PR's own run. On PR #2429, every Platform CI job found its services healthy at the runner's first 2 s check; container start-up fell from 21 to 27 s to 10 to 15 s, test shards from 117 to 146 s to 104 to 114 s, and checks from 98 to 124 s to 94 s. (5) The platform-ci-test cache is 835 MB, and its restore takes 18 s (6.6 s download, 11.8 s extract), because the environment carries Playwright's full Chromium (403 MB) beside the headless shell the suite launches (273 MB). That set is steward Story 131.1's ruling, so changing it is the operator's call, not a fix. (6) spec-surface reported no drift for the two workflow files: neither is in a Spec's surface, so no memlog line or stamp applies.
